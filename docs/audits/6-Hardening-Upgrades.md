Six Hardening Upgrades Against Modern & Unforeseen SteganalysisTo survive both classical calibration attacks and modern spatial/frequency steganalysis (JRM, DCTR, GFR, SRNet), upgrade Stages 2, 4, 5, and 6 of your pipeline with these mechanisms:1. Asymmetric Model-Preserving Distortion ($\rho^+ \neq \rho^-$) instead of Naive Tie-BreakingStandard J-UNIWARD computes a single symmetric cost $\rho_i$ for modifying coefficient $c_i$ by $\pm 1$, meaning costPlus === costMinus is always true before PRNG noise.How to upgrade juniward.js: Modulate $\rho^+$ and $\rho^-$ using the empirical histogram ratio of the carrier itself (or a generalized Cauchy/Laplacian prior) so that moving toward zero is slightly cheaper than moving away from zero on $\vert{}c\vert{} \ge 2$, while moving $0 \to \pm 1$ and $\pm 1 \to 0$ are forbidden (WET_COST), or scale $\rho^{\text{away}}$ by $(h(\vert{}c\vert{}) / h(\vert{}c\vert{}+1))^\gamma$. This preserves the exact first-order exponential decay of the DCT histogram without sacrificing J-UNIWARD's spatial wavelet adaptivity.2. Fix the "Anti-SCA PRNG Noise" Side-Channel in Step 5In Step 5, your diagram notes: "Evaluates additive distortion costs $\rho^+$ and $\rho^-$ per AC coefficient, perturbed by anti-SCA PRNG noise."The Danger: In J-UNIWARD, costs $\rho_i$ are computed solely from the cover/stego image so the receiver does not need the costs to extract the message (the receiver only computes $H \cdot y = m$). However, if you perturb $\rho_i$ with PRNG noise during embedding:If the PRNG is seeded with a fixed or unkeyed seed, an attacker can simulate your exact embedding footprint.If the PRNG is seeded with your stego key, make sure the amplitude of the perturbation is tiny (e.g., $\le 1\%$ multiplicative jitter) purely to break ties in smooth/saturated regions. Large random perturbations override J-UNIWARD's wavelet sensitivity $\xi(r, c)$ and push modifications into flat sky/skin blocks where deep-learning detectors (SRNet) have near-100% detection accuracy.3. Eliminate Fixed-Bucket Payload Length Quantization Peaks (Step 4)Step 4 states: "Wraps chunk payload into fixed CSPRNG-padded buckets (256 B – 128 KB)."The Unforeseen Attack: Academic & forensic wardens train "quantitative steganalysis" (payload-size estimators) across batches of images. If every carrier you produce has an embedded bit-length equal to an exact power-of-2 bucket ($256\text{ B}$, $512\text{ B}$, $1024\text{ B}$), a batch warden estimates the change rate $\hat{\beta}$ across your carriers and detects discrete quantization spikes.The Upgrade: Since you have zero cleartext length headers on the wire, how does the extractor know which bucket size to extract?Instead of padding to a static byte size ($256\text{ B}, 512\text{ B}$), size the STC syndrome extraction length as a deterministic keyed fraction of each carrier's unique bpnzAC count (derived via HKDF from the carrier's immutable structural metadata + key, or bounded by the specific shard tier). Because every JPEG has a slightly different bpnzAC count, the wire bit-length varies continuously from image to image while remaining 100% deterministic to a key-holder.4. Sub-Band & Chrominance Cost Penalty ($Y$ vs. $Cb / Cr$ and High-Frequency DC/AC)Standard J-UNIWARD evaluates wavelet residuals on a single luminance channel, or treats $Y, Cb, Cr$ independently.The Vulnerability: Modern color JPEG steganalysis (like CRM / J-CRM and color-SRNet) aggressively targets chrominance ($Cb, Cr$) AC modifications and high-frequency $8 \times 8$ diagonal AC modes (indices $u + v \ge 9$), which are almost entirely $0$ in natural $4:2:0$ JPEGs.The Upgrade:Restrict embedding strictly to the Luminance ($Y$) channel, or apply a heavy multiplier ($3\times\text{ to }5\times$ distortion penalty) to $Cb/Cr$ AC coefficients.Apply a frequency-mode weight multiplier based on the quantization table step $Q_{u,v}$: if $Q_{u,v}$ is large (high frequencies), a $\pm 1$ change in the quantized coefficient produces a massive spatial step of $\pm Q_{u,v}$ during IDCT, lighting up spatial rich-model filters (DCTR/Gabor). Wait—J-UNIWARD already multiplies the wavelet impact by $Q_{u,v}$, make sure your juniward.js scales the unit DCT basis response by $Q_{u,v}$! (A common implementation bug in custom J-UNIWARD ports is using unquantized unit DCT basis blocks instead of $Q_{u,v}$-scaled basis blocks, which drastically under-penalizes high-frequency DCT modes.)5. Inter-Block De-Clustering (De-Correlating $8 \times 8$ Spatial Neighbors)J-UNIWARD is an additive distortion model: it assumes modifying coefficient $A$ and adjacent coefficient $B$ has cost $\rho_A + \rho_B$. In reality, modifying two coefficients in the same $8 \times 8$ MCU block (or across an $8 \times 8$ block boundary) creates non-additive constructive interference in the Daubechies wavelet domain.The Upgrade (Cmd-J-UNIWARD / Clustering Penalty): You don't need full Gibbs sampling to get 80% of the benefit of non-additive steganography. Either:One-Change-Per-Block Cap (or Block Striding): When building the candidate pool for the STC trellis, if payload density is low ($\le 0.05\text{ bpnzAC}$ means at most 1 change per ~20 non-zero AC coefficients, or fewer than 1 change per two $8 \times 8$ blocks), you can enforce at most 1–2 candidate slots per $8 \times 8$ block, ORTrellis Sub-Block Interleaving: Because your Feistel permutation $\pi_{\text{seed}, N}(t)$ already scrambles the coordinate order across the entire image, any local trellis window of width $w$ automatically spans spatially distant $8 \times 8$ blocks! Verify that you apply $\pi_{\text{seed}, N}(t)$ before feeding costs into the STC trellis, rather than running STC sequentially on spatial blocks and permuting only the message bits. (Your Step 6 diagram says "Scrambles coefficient traversal order using Keyed Cycle-Walking Feistel permutation", which is the exact right order—just double-check the code actually permutes both coeffs and rhoPlus/rhoMinus by identical Feistel indices before stcEmbed).6. Proportional Distortion Allocation Across Multi-Carrier Batches (Step 2)Look at Step 2 of your flow: "Scans carrier non-zero AC coefficients (bpnzAC capacity metric). Assigns proportional erasure chunks across carriers."The Unforeseen Attack (Batch Steganalysis / Pooling): Allocating payload strictly proportional to bpnzAC (constant embedding rate) is known in batch steganalysis as the "even rate" strategy, which is sub-optimal when one carrier is a smooth sunset photo (high bpnzAC from gentle gradients, but high distortion per bit) and another is a dense forest trail photo (complex texture, ultra-low J-UNIWARD distortion).The Upgrade (Image-Merging / Constant Marginal Distortion): Instead of allocating erasure chunk sizes strictly by bpnzAC count, weight the capacity of each carrier $k$ by its total inverse J-UNIWARD cost mass:$$W_k = \sum_{i \in \text{NZAC}_k} \frac{1}{\rho_{k, i}}$$This automatically shifts heavier erasure shards (or higher share density) into highly textured carriers and starves smooth carriers, minimizing the maximum detectability across the entire batch.
